# Reference narrative and copy structure: "Solution Wagon" 9:16 ad, rebuilt as a FILMX script

Source: `filmx-ref/ref.mov`. Scratch work is in `filmx-ref/work/narrative/`:

* `edit_order_sheet.png`: one frame per beat, in true edit order.
* `sheet_00..03.png`: contact sheets every 0.25 s, recording time.
* `s_*.png`: 15 fps strips of every text reveal.
* `tl.txt`: cut and sharpness timeline.

This report covers **what is said, in what order, and why**. For motion and design numbers, see `motion.md` and `design.md`. They agree with the timings below to within ±1 frame.

## 0. Findings

1. **The ad is an order-of-operations argument.**
   * Every line is about sequence: *start*, *begin here*, *but before*, *need to be clear*, *without those*, *turns … into*.
   * The thesis is "do X **before** Y". It sells a process, not a product, and never mentions a price, an offer or a CTA verb.
2. **It is built from two nouns.** An enemy term (**Ads**) and a hero term (**Clarity**) are set up in the first 3.7 s.
   * Both are repeated **verbatim** later, with no synonyms:
     * "Ads": 3 times on screen in the video (0.3 s, 10.6 s, 21.7 s), plus once on the poster (the player's cover frame, not part of the edit).
     * "Clarity": 2 times (2.6 s and 25.7 s).
   * The last idea is a callback to the second one.
3. **Arc and runtime share:**

   | Act | Share of 33.25 s |
   |---|---|
   | Contrarian hook | 11 % |
   | Status-quo montage | 20 % |
   | Pivot | 5 % |
   | Checklist of 3 questions | 22 % |
   | Stakes | 17 % |
   | Reframe | 13 % |
   | Brand | 11 % |

   The argument itself (acts 3–6) is **56 %** of the runtime. The brand gets 11 %.
4. **Each idea gets exactly one monochrome object that acts out the sentence.**
   * Rocket = ads (hype, launch).
   * Magnifier = clarity (it literally sharpens the word).
   * Card = budget.
   * Juggled app cubes = platforms.
   * Busy office ring = campaigns.
   * Thinking statue = answers.
   * Ad-platform icons = ads.
   * Puzzle = activity.
   * Lever = leverage.
   * **Noise ideas get many objects** (juggled cubes, a ring full of people, two icons). **Truth ideas get one calm object** (one lens, one lever).
   * **The pivot and the framework get no object at all**: only words on paper.
5. **Idea pacing is fast, then slow, then medium, then slow.**
   * Montage: one noun per 1.1–1.5 s.
   * Checklist: one question per 1.5–1.8 s, inside a single 7.2 s shot.
   * Stakes: one claim per 1.4–2.2 s.
   * Reframe: one sentence over 3.9 s.
   * VO decelerates toward the resolution: the hook is spoken at about 4 words/s and the final line at about 2 words/s.
6. **For FILMX the skeleton transfers almost one to one:**
   * "We don't start with ads" becomes «ما نبدأ بالسعر… نبدأ بالأصل». FILMX genuinely doesn't publish prices and quotes only after inspection, so the hook is also true.
   * The 3 questions become 3 genuine-vs-fake tests. The third question ("and what happens after…") becomes «ووش يصير لو تضرر؟».
   * One new act, **"answer pills"**, is inserted between the reframe and the brand card. It answers the 3 questions with FILMX's real guarantees:
     * XPEL / Ravoony films
     * official warranty of up to 10 years, registered to the car's plate number
     * free replacement of any damaged piece, for any reason, with no conditions
     * 30-day installation warranty

### Copy mechanics worth reusing

* **The enemy and hero nouns are fixed and repeated verbatim.**
  * "Ads" appears at 0.27, 10.6 and 21.67 s, plus once on the poster (the cover frame).
  * "Clarity" appears at 2.6 and 25.7 s.
  * "Marketing" appears at 4.0 and 26.4 s.
  * No synonyms are ever used, so the argument feels inevitable.
* **The sequence vocabulary runs through every act:** *start*, *start with*, *begin here*, *before*, *need to be*, *without*, *turns into*. The whole ad is an "order of operations".
* **Colour has grammar.**
  * GREY giant = the *topic* noun (Ads, Clarity, Marketing, Answers).
  * INK bold = the *verdict* (Start with, Conversations, Growth, Activity, Into leverage).
  * Small lead-ins carry the grammar (We don't, Most, Without those, They create, Turns).
* **Line breaks follow meaning, not width.** Every beat is 1–3 stacked chunks, each chunk one syntactic unit, and the hero chunk is always a single noun.
* **Questions sit only in the framework. Statements are everywhere else.** The only direct "you" is in pill 2.
* **The brand name never appears in the argument.** It sits as a constant header bug top-centre, and at full size only on the end card.
* **Text leads voice.**
  * Each line is fully legible **0.0–0.35 s before** its word is spoken (Answers 0.33 s, Activity 0.23 s, Clarity 0.15 s, Into leverage 0.20 s).
  * The strike on "Growth" finishes **0.15 s before** VO says "growth": the visual verdict lands first.
* **Cuts sit in VO gaps.** The cut comes ~0.3–0.4 s after a phrase ends, and the next phrase starts ~0.3–0.4 s after the cut. Examples: VO gap 21.22–21.90 has cut 21.60; gap 23.32–23.95 has cut 23.80; gap 25.22–26.15 has the crash-zoom at 25.2–25.5.
* **The VO decelerates.** Hook ≈4 words/s. Stakes phrases ≈2.8 w/s with 0.6–0.7 s gaps. Final line ≈2.0 w/s, followed by a 0.6 s tail before the burn.

## 4. Visual-metaphor system

| Idea | Object | What the object *does* (the verb) | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ads (enemy) | Chrome rocket | Launches past you, fast | Symbol of hype |
| Clarity (hero) | Magnifying glass | Slides in and **magnifies the word itself** | Classical symbol |
| Marketing conversations begin here | Paper-cut silhouette with a blank sheet | Cranes up and presents an empty page | Anonymous human |
| Budget | Metal credit card | Drifts diagonally | Literal |
| Platforms | Hand juggling app cubes | Many things in the air | Literal, **plural = noise** |
| Campaign ideas | ∞ ring cut away into offices | Busy people inside the machine | Literal/architectural, **plural** |
| But before ads… | *Nothing* | Stillness | Absence = pause |
| 3 things need to be clear | *Nothing* (pills only) | Pills unfold one by one | Pure information = clarity |
| Without those answers | Marble thinker at a laptop | Thinks and types | Classical + modern |
| Ads don't create growth | Meta and Google Ads tiles, plus a strike | Levitate, then the word is struck | Literal plus negation mark |
| They create activity | Hands with puzzle pieces | Pieces pushed together, no picture | Metaphor of busy-work |
| Clarity → leverage | Seesaw: tiny ball vs heavy sphere | Heavy mass slowly lifts | Classical physics metaphor |
| Brand | Dark card, only colour | Logo writes on | – |

Rules the reference follows:

1. **One idea = one object = one verb.** The object performs the sentence's verb, not its noun.
2. **The ladder runs from literal to symbolic.** Tactics (budget, platforms, ad tools) are literal objects. Principles (clarity, answers, leverage) are classical symbols: lens, statue, lever.
3. **Plural for noise, singular for truth.** The status-quo beats are crowded (cubes, office ring, two tiles). The truth beats have one object and empty paper around it.
4. **Anonymised humans only.** A silhouette, a statue, hands. **No realistic face.** The ad never risks the uncanny valley. This is directly relevant to the user's complaint that the previous FILMX character "looks unreal".
5. **The two most important beats have no object** (pivot and framework). Words alone signal "this is the point".
6. **The enemy object comes back in a weaker form.** The heroic rocket at 0 s returns as two flat app icons whose promise is struck out at 21.6 s.

## 7. The same structure as a FILMX script (Saudi dialect, 9:16, ≈40–44 s)

Key nouns, fixed and repeated verbatim:
* **Enemy:** «السعر» (price-first buying is how people end up with fake film).
* **Hero:** «الأصل». It is a deliberate double meaning: *the genuine/original* and *the fundamental*. «أصول» also means *assets*, which suits the Old Money register.
* **Antithesis pair:** «حماية» vs «لمعة». Real protection vs a temporary shine, mirroring Growth vs Activity.

* The reference's GREY hero word becomes **gold-muted #8A6E3F**.
* INK verdicts are **#1C1B18**.
* Lead-ins are ink at about 70 %.
* The strike-through is drawn **right → left** (RTL reading direction).

## 10. Arabic copy rules derived from the reference

1. **Line length:** at most 3 chunks per beat and at most 5 words per beat.
   * The hero chunk is one noun of **≤6 letters** («السعر», «الأصل», «حماية», «اللمعة»), so it can be set at 47–50 % frame width.
   * Pills may run to ≤7 words (the reference's longest pill has 7).
2. **Fix the enemy and hero nouns and repeat them verbatim.** Enemy «السعر»: at least 2 times. Hero «الأصل»: at least 3 times (hook, reframe, VO sign-off). No synonyms.
3. **Use the sequence vocabulary**, because the argument is an order: «نبدأ»، «تبدأ من هنا»، «بس قبل»، «قبل ما تركّب»، «بدون»، «يحوّل… إلى».
4. **Questions only in the framework pills, answers only in the proof pills.** "You" («سيارتك», «تركّب») appears mainly inside the pills.
5. **Negate visually.** Strike the promised noun («حماية») R→L over ≈0.6 s, finishing about 0.15 s before the VO speaks it.
6. **Text leads voice by 0.0–0.35 s. Cut 0.3–0.4 s after a phrase ends.**
7. **No hype vocabulary.** No «عرض خاص», «لفترة محدودة», «!!» or price numbers.
   * Offers are phrased as facts: «نبدّلها مجاناً · بلا شروط».
   * Numerals are Arabic-Indic («١٠», «٣٠») in Arabic lines. Brand names stay Latin (XPEL, Ravoony, FILMX).
8. **Animate per word or line, never per letter** (it would break Arabic joining).
   * The typewriter pivot is the one exception. Type by character: RTL typing keeps each word's joined form correct, and it naturally reproduces the reference's "cursor at the left edge" look.
9. **Brand name placement:** a constant small header bug. The name is not spoken until the proof act («في فيلم إكس») and the sign-off.
